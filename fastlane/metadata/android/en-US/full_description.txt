Tally is a budget tracker that keeps itself up to date.

Indian banks and UPI apps already text you every time money moves. Tally reads
those messages on your phone, works out the amount, the merchant and the
account, and files the transaction. You set a spending limit for the period and
Tally tells you where you stand against it — not just how much is gone, but
whether that is early or late for the day you are on.

<b>Everything stays on your phone.</b>
Tally has no internet permission at all. There is no account to make, no server
to sync with, no analytics and no ads. Your ledger is one database file on the
device; uninstalling deletes it.

<b>What it does</b>

* Reads bank and UPI transaction SMS and records them automatically. Messages
  that are not about a transaction are skipped and never stored.
* Tracks several accounts and credit cards, matched by the last four digits
  each bank quotes. It notices accounts it has never seen and offers to add
  them.
* Learns your categories. Label a merchant once and Tally applies it to past
  and future transactions — no bundled language model, just a small lookup
  table and some counting.
* One spending limit per period, with a pace marker so you can see whether you
  are ahead or behind for the day of the month.
* Charts the period by category and day by day.
* Imports CSV and PDF bank statements to seed history.
* Exports the whole ledger to CSV whenever you want it.
* Follows your system light and dark theme.

<b>Permissions</b>

READ_SMS is used for one thing: parsing transaction messages on the device so
the ledger fills itself in. It is optional — you can skip it entirely and use
manual entry and statement import instead. Nothing is transmitted anywhere.
